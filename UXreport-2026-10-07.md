# CoreValley — UI/UX Swarm Audit Report

*Date: 7 October 2026. Six-agent audit (Novice, Power User, Accessibility, CRO & Copy, Visual & Interaction, Mobile & Technical) of the redesigned CoreValley site, followed by a multi-agent implementation pass. Part A is the audit as delivered. Part B records what was changed afterwards.*

---

# Part A — Comprehensive UI/UX Swarm Audit Report

## 1. Executive Summary

- **Scope.** The input fields in the audit template were empty, so context was taken from the repo: **CoreValley**, a GPU cloud in Kathmandu (H100/H200, billed in NPR, data kept in Nepal). Audience, from `/use-cases` and `/company`: ML engineers and startups, university labs, and regulated buyers (banks, hospitals, government). Primary goal: early-access requests via `/contact`. Method: read the source, ran the working tree locally, reviewed screenshots of home, products, a product page, pricing, contact and platform at 1440×900 and 390×844 in the default light theme, and measured contrast, tap targets and form state in the browser.
- **Overall:** the site looks expensive and the copy is unusually honest and specific. Its problem was not polish. It contradicted itself about what is real today, and the conversion path broke at the moments a prospect commits.

**Top 3 critical flaws**
1. **Availability and claims contradict each other.**
   - The hero said "coming online", but the contact band said "h200 · available now".
   - `/products` labelled every GPU "coming soon", including H100 and H200.
   - The RTX PRO 6000 headlined "Hopper and Blackwell, side by side" with no availability label.
   - The stats claimed "11s cold start… measured", and the rack graphic implied 180 GPUs.
   - The company page promises "where a GPU has not landed yet, we say so". The homepage didn't.
2. **The conversion path breaks.**
   - The quote console promised "list prices" and showed none.
   - "Request this plan · carries this configuration to the form" carried nothing (verified: the form loaded empty).
   - On phones the dials blocked page scrolling.
   - The contact form started 1,182 px down the page on a phone.
3. **The pricing page undercut the "predictable NPR pricing" promise.** "Clear pricing, in rupees." sat directly above "engineering placeholders… Not for quoting." Pricing was missing from the header, yet linked from the footer and every product page.

**Top 3 strengths**
1. **Copy discipline.** Lines are concrete, checkable and local: "Built for the lab that shares two cards between forty students"; "A run that dies at 02:00 NPT is answered from Kathmandu…".
2. **A distinctive, coherent visual system.** The terrain hero, the token palette and the one-primary-button rule signal engineering competence.
3. **Accessibility basics above the norm.** Every effect respects reduced motion. There's a skip link, `<main>` takes focus, the dials are real `spinbutton`s with `aria-valuetext`, and count-ups render final values on the server.

**Where the agents disagreed (resolved for the user)**
- **Visual showpieces vs. phones.** Keep the hero terrain and the `/platform` dive; simplify mid-page effects on touch devices.
- **Price table vs. trust.** Publish the billing *model* now and the rates once approved.
- **Badges vs. honesty.** Drop "SOC 2 … alignment"; use real facts a buyer can check.

**What couldn't be assessed:** no analytics is installed (no bounce, scroll-depth or funnel data); which GPUs early-access teams can actually use; sources for the 11 s and hydropower claims; the timeline for approved rates; real-device performance.

## 2. The Swarm Analysis (Agent Findings)

### 👤 Agent 1: The Novice (First Impressions & Clarity)
- **The 5-second test is only a half-pass.** "The valley is coming online." is a metaphor; it could be an ISP, a tourism board or a launch countdown. "GPU" first appears in line 2. "Nepal" only appears in line 3, in 15 px grey text, at a reading grade of about 14 — the hardest sentence in the hero.
- **I can't tell whether this exists.** "Coming online" plus "Get early access" says I can't use it yet; then "available now", H100/H200 chips, and an 11-second cold start "measured".
- **Which GPU would I get?** Chips say "H100 / H200"; the hardware section compares H200 with RTX PRO 6000 and never mentions the H100; `/products` says every GPU is coming soon.
- **A wall of jargon.** 30+ distinct technical terms on the homepage (MIG, HAMi, vCluster, Cilium, eBPF, CRDs, RBAC, IPMI, LiteLLM, SXM5, NVDEC, 2g.35gb…), almost none explained there. "np-ktm-1" appears at least six times, never introduced.
- **The plain-language payoff comes late.** "Pay in rupees, your data stays here, someone in Kathmandu answers" lands about 4 screens down on desktop and 5 on a phone, set in monospace.
- **What works:** the hero chips are the clearest thing above the fold.

### 🏃 Agent 2: The Power User (Navigation & Efficiency)
- **No Pricing link in the header or mobile menu.** Cloud buyers go to pricing first.
- **The quickest route to a quote throws away the input.** The console link added `?gpu=&count=&hours=`; `/contact` never read it.
- **The dials move one step at a time.** L4 to a full H200 took 9 wheel ticks or drags.
- **Real-looking links lead to stubs.** Footer "API reference" and "Quickstart" open "In progress" pages; the homepage shows a CLI with no install page.
- **Product names change between site and docs.** GPU pods ↔ "GPU Workspace", JupyterHub ↔ "AI Lab", Model endpoints ↔ "Inference API".
- **No way to sign in** for invited early-access users.
- **The pricing table offers no guidance.** 12 configurations, no "pick this if…"; `h200 · 7g.141gb` (NPR 405) vs `h200 · 1x` (NPR 415) unexplained.
- **What works:** product pages are excellent for this persona; pricing toggles work.

### ♿ Agent 3: Accessibility & Inclusion
- **Contrast (measured, light theme).** 14 text elements failed AA, all `--ink-600` #8B95A3 on #F3F5F7 = **2.77:1** at 9.5–11 px. Inactive pinned-story steps sat at `opacity-40` ≈ **1.9:1**. Dark mode worse: ink-500 ≈4.1:1, ink-600 ≈2.5:1.
- **Buttons nested inside links.** Every CTA was `<Link><Button>` (17 instances): invalid HTML, two tab stops, announced twice.
- **Invisible focus targets.** Below 1024 px the closed mobile menu was only collapsed, not inert — 6 invisible links plus a button in the tab order.
- **Small touch targets.** 36×36 menu button ~10 px from a 30×30 theme toggle; footer links 18 px tall.
- **Reading load.** Sovereignty argument (~136 words) set in mono; every label uppercase with wide tracking.
- **Sensory load.** About a dozen canvas/WebGL surfaces plus marquee and spinning dials; the perspective grid behind body text everywhere.
- **Theme.** OS dark-mode preference ignored by design.
- **Done well:** reduced motion everywhere, skip link, `aria-valuetext`, `aria-hidden` ticker duplicate, server-rendered numbers.

### ✍️ Agent 4: CRO & Copywriting (Text & Conversion)
- **Status is the product right now, and it's the least clear message.** One truthful line, repeated everywhere.
- **Unverifiable claims cost more trust than they earn:** "11s … measured", "72 gpus" rack labels, unconfirmed hydropower, "SOC 2 Type I framework alignment" (the console's own mock data says no audit has been completed).
- **The sovereignty promise breaks at the first form submission.** The enquiry form posts to `formsubmit.co`, a third-party relay not mentioned in the privacy policy.
- **Expectations set too late.** The reply time only appeared after sending.
- **Three framings in one flow:** "Let's discuss your workloads." / "Request early access" / "Send message".
- **The quote console looked unfinished:** "list prices" in the bar, an empty screen panel.
- **Thin trust signals:** no named people, facility facts, dated progress or phone number.
- **Keep:** "Sovereign by construction, not by contract." · "You rent the GPU. We run the rest." · "Rent a slice. Rent a rack. Same control plane."

### 🎨 Agent 5: Visual & Interaction Design
- **Strengths:** tight token system, consistent eyebrows and rules, one filled button per view, beautiful GPU comparison cards, memorable hero terrain.
- **Too many showpieces:** 8+ animated or 3D systems on a page 10.6 screens tall (16.6 on a phone).
- **Text over texture:** terrain dots through the stats labels; perspective grid behind tables and paragraphs.
- **Unfinished-looking spaces:** empty quote-console panel; a hole in the 3-column "soon" row; a ~150 px dead gap above the ticker.
- **Hierarchy:** uppercase tracked mono everywhere; headings wrapping into 3–4 lines beside empty half-rows.
- **`/platform` ending:** the point cloud rendered through the translucent footer.
- **Small errors:** H100 dial said "hbm2e" (catalog: HBM3); chip-graph labels clipped on phones.

### 📱 Agent 6: Mobile & Technical UX
- **Scroll traps:** `.drum { touch-action: none }` across ~480 px of a phone; wheel hijack on desktop.
- **No early-access CTA for ~12,000 px**; header CTA hidden below 768 px.
- **The pinned story fought the screen:** the sticky rack covered a third of the viewport and hid step headings.
- **CLI chips wrapped mid-flag.**
- **Contact form 1,182 px down.**
- **Weight:** about a dozen canvas/WebGL surfaces plus three.js, unmeasured on mid-range Android.
- **What works:** no horizontal overflow at 390 px, clean drawer, scrolling tables.

## 4. Text & Content Deep Dive
- **Tone of voice.** Engineer to engineer, terse, anti-hype; British spelling consistent. Two registers: homepage for platform engineers, `/use-cases` and `/company` in plain business language — under-serving university heads and regulated enterprises. Fix by leading each block with the outcome in plain words.
- **Readability.** Estimated Flesch-Kincaid: hero support line ≈14, product cards ≈7.5, sovereignty list ≈7.8, architecture cards ≈10.8 — the formula undercounts acronyms. Repetition: "without changing provider, currency or jurisdiction" ×4; NPR ≥7× on the homepage; "np-ktm-1" ≥6× undefined.
- **Information architecture.** Three overlapping "underneath" sections; ambiguous "Products" vs "Platform"; Pricing missing from the header. Suggested header: Products · Pricing · Use cases · Docs · Company.

## 5. Actionable Roadmap (Prioritized)
- 🚀 **Quick Wins:** one truthful status line; real quote → form handoff; free the dials; hide placeholder rates and add Pricing to the nav; form first on mobile with a reply-time promise; mobile header CTA and 44 px menu; accessibility batch (`inert`, `ButtonLink`, contrast, step dimming); remove "SOC 2 … alignment" and disclose FormSubmit.
- 🏗️ **Strategic Projects:** trust content for regulated buyers (people, facility, SLA, phone, in-country form endpoint); docs before power users; approved rates with guidance; homepage diet and a mobile performance budget; sign-in and status page when real.
- 🧹 **Housekeeping:** H100 label, "soon" grid, chip-graph clipping, iceberg/footer overlap, layer-06 claim, define "np-ktm-1", body font for prose, required-field legend, ticker gap, consider OS colour preference.
- 🗑️ **Deprioritize:** re-enabling the latency map; more 3D set pieces; status link before monitoring; a logo wall before customers; rewriting the voice wholesale.

## 6. Final Verdict
CoreValley already does the hard part: it looks and sounds like a team that knows its hardware, and its advantages (rupees, data residency, support in Nepal time) are distinctive and well argued. What held it back was credibility and a broken quote-to-form handoff, not aesthetics. An early-access site sells a promise, so the promise has to be the most consistent thing on the page. The key is the site's own copy rule: every claim should be a number, a component name, or something a skeptical bank engineer can check.

# Part B — What was done afterwards (7 Oct 2026)

## Confirmed facts used
- NVIDIA H200: **available now**, through **enterprise early access**.
- H100 and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell: **coming soon**; L40S and L4 after them.
- Latency: **under 5 ms in Kathmandu**. The datacenter runs on **hydropower**.
- Rates: **not published yet**; every request gets a firm NPR quote within one working day.
- Single source of truth: `lib/availability.ts` (status wording, facts) and `lib/catalog.ts` (GPU status).

## Research highlights (web research agent)
- The hero direction passes Harry Dry's tests (picture it, check it, nobody else can say it). Closest models: Mistral Compute, Exoscale, Infomaniak.
- Hidden prices read as evasive (NN/g); explain the billing model and make the quote fast and firm. The one-working-day promise is a differentiator — none of the GPU clouds checked promise a response time.
- Use exactly two availability labels and define "early access" in writing.
- Trust without customers: a real-organisation block, named people, facility facts with sources, a data-residency explainer, `security.txt`, public docs.
- Mobile: value and CTA on the first screen; at most 3 required form fields; labels above fields.
- Plain language: explain each term on first use, split sentences over 25 words, avoid idioms.
- Full brief: `RESEARCH.md` in the session scratchpad.

## Changes implemented
- **Homepage:** hero "The valley is online." with a plain subhead ("Nepal's own GPU cloud: NVIDIA H200s, rented by the second from a hydro-powered Kathmandu datacenter."), a plain-text status kicker, reply-time microcopy, plain chips and stats (141 GB, <5 ms, 100%, NPR); section order Why → early-access band → Platform → Hardware → final CTA with "what happens next" and the quote console. Removed: 11 s stat, SOC 2 line, implied fleet sizes, PolicyGrid, the latency map, the dotted section dividers, and (at the owner's request) the "Under the hood" section.
- **Growth-path graphic:** the 3D rack row was replaced by isometric line art (one H200 with 2 of 7 slices → 4 whole cards → a reserved rack) drawn in the chip-graph style; non-sticky row on phones.
- **Hardware:** "The H200 today. Blackwell next." with status labels on each card; specs fold behind "Full specs" on phones.
- **Quote console:** H200 options first, coming-soon GPUs flagged; no prices; plan panel with status, what you get, reply time and billing; wheel only when focused; `pan-y` on touch; configuration now prefills the contact form.
- **Contact:** "Tell us what you want to run."; form first on phones; sentence-case labels; "* required"; FormSubmit disclosed under the button, in the privacy policy and on the data-residency page; fallback returns to the site.
- **Pricing:** placeholder rate tables hidden while rates are unapproved; "Billed by the second, in rupees."; plain billing explainer, three ways to rent, FAQ incl. "When will you publish rates?".
- **Products, company, use cases, platform, docs, SEO:** plain-language rewrites, correct statuses everywhere, "What does early access mean?" FAQ, one product vocabulary across site and docs, updated titles and descriptions, `SEO_roadmap.txt` and README notes.
- **System and accessibility:** Pricing in the nav; mobile header CTA; 44 px menu; `inert` closed drawer with Escape; `ButtonLink` replacing 17 nested link/buttons; contrast tokens fixed in both themes; calmer grid floor; no pills or blinking dots; footer links only to real content plus a Security (`security.txt`) link.
- **Console mock:** H200-only workloads; H100 and others "coming soon"; honest SOC 2 wording; accessible mobile drawer.

## Verification (production build of the working tree)
- 51 static pages built; docs export check passed (8/8).
- QA sweep, 10 pages × 2 widths: 0 contrast failures, no horizontal overflow, 0 nested interactive elements, no scroll traps, no banned phrases or unconfirmed claims, clean console, fonts loading.
- `tsc` clean; ESLint 0 errors in site code.
