# 01 · Vision and story

> Read this first. Every later decision — a colour, a spring, a sentence — should be traceable back to something on this page.

## 1. What we are building

A website for **CoreValley**, the GPU cloud being built in the Kathmandu Valley. The site has two jobs:

1. **Persuade.** A CTO in Kathmandu, a research lead at a university, a bank's head of IT, an Indian startup founder, an investor, and a future engineer should each leave believing *this is a serious infrastructure company, and it is right here*.
2. **Inform.** Each of those people should find exactly what they need — what we sell, what it runs on, where the data lives, how billing works, how to start — without wading through things that belong to someone else.

The ambition is an **Awwwards Site of the Year-calibre experience** that also scores high on usability, accessibility and developer quality. Most award sites trade usability for spectacle. We do not get to make that trade: we sell infrastructure, and infrastructure buyers punish anything that wastes their time.

## 2. The north star

> **The valley is coming online.**

The sentence the owner already loves is the whole concept. A valley — a place, with mountains around it, rivers through it and people in it — is switching on. Power comes down from the snow. It becomes compute. Compute becomes tokens, answers, models, products. And it all happens *here*, inside the rim of the valley, under Nepal's own clock (UTC+05:45).

Every page is a step on that journey. Every animation shows something switching on, flowing, or being contained.

## 3. The three visual ideas (and nothing else)

Award juries reward coherence over quantity. The site uses **three** visual ideas, everywhere, and refuses the rest.

| Idea | What it is | What it means | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Dot** | A field of points — a dot matrix — that can form a mountain, a logo, a number, a GPU, a map, a word | Compute is countable, granular, sliceable; the brand mark is made of dots | Hero valley, Living Mark (logo), Reservoir footer, generative blog covers, numbers, 404 |
| **The Flow** | Light or particles moving along a path: river, cable, token stream | Energy → compute → answers; things moving through the valley | Snow → Token journey, page transitions (Fog pass), the Meter, latency pulses |
| **The Glow** | A soft, living gradient edge (the "Siri glow") in hydro green → glacier cyan → dusk violet | *A machine is working right now* | Only while something real is happening: a form submitting, a demo request streaming, a model answering. Never as decoration |

Anything that is not a Dot, a Flow or a Glow — glassy cards, stock photography, random gradients, generic 3D blobs — is out.

## 4. Principles

1. **Every effect carries a fact.** If an animation does not encode a true statement (a size, a speed, a boundary, a state), it is cut. The valley glows where the river runs; the meter stops when the job stops; the dataset bounces off the valley rim because the data really does stay inside.
2. **Truth first, then beauty.** No number appears without a source and a date. No claim we cannot defend. "Coming soon" says coming soon. Honesty is a design feature here — competitors hide their caveats; we show them beautifully.
3. **One idea per screen.** Information arrives one thought at a time, with space around it, so a visitor remembers it. A metric gets a whole moment, a comparison and a footnote — not a row in a stat strip.
4. **Each page owns its subject.** Products talk about products. The company page talks about the company. A fact lives in exactly one home; elsewhere it is a one-line pointer.
5. **Alive, never noisy.** The site breathes when idle, answers the cursor and the thumb, and moves continuously with scroll — but at any moment there is one thing moving that matters, not ten.
6. **Light and dark are both first-class.** Two complete themes, designed separately, not one inverted from the other.
7. **The phone is not a fallback.** Mobile gets its own choreography built on touch, scroll velocity and (opt-in) tilt — not a smaller desktop with effects turned off.
8. **Fast is part of beautiful.** A page that stutters is ugly. We budget every millisecond and every kilobyte, and we degrade gracefully, never abruptly.
9. **Respect the visitor.** Reduced motion, keyboard use, screen readers, slow networks, data-saver and low battery are designed states, not afterthoughts.
10. **Earned delight.** Delight is the result of the other nine principles done well, not confetti added at the end.

## 5. The emotional arc of a visit

Designing a journey means designing feelings in order:

| Moment | Visitor feels | How we get there |
|---|---|---|
| First second | *Oh.* Curiosity, a little awe | The valley assembles from dots and the headline rises; the word *online* lights up |
| First scroll | *This is different* | The camera dips into the valley; the page moves with them, not at them |
| Middle | *I understand this* | One fact at a time: what an H200 is, how it can be sliced, where the data lives, how billing works |
| Interaction | *I'm in control* | They slice a GPU, stop the meter, try to drag data out of the valley — and the site answers instantly |
| Decision | *This is safe and serious* | Clear products, clear specs, honest status, named partners (HPE, NVIDIA), a real quote promise |
| Action | *That was easy* | A request form that feels like a short conversation and ends with a personal plan card |
| Leaving | *I'll remember that* | The footer logo assembles from dots and ripples under their cursor; Nepal's clock ticks in the corner |

## 7. Brand evolution (what stays, what changes)

**Stays (owner decision):** the CoreValley logo mark and wordmark; **hydro green** as the one brand accent.

**Evolves:**
- A proper **light theme** ("Glacier") and **dark theme** ("Night Valley"), each complete. No more black islands on a white page.
- **Typography** with Apple's discipline: one sans for everything (tight display tracking, comfortable body), one mono for data.
- **Imagery** is generated, not bought: dot-matrix terrain, contour prints, procedural GPUs and racks drawn by us. Real photography only of real people and real places we have permission to show, commissioned later.
- **Motion language** becomes a brand asset with tokens, like colour.

**Retires:** stock photos (all five Unsplash images), zinc/black slab bands, glass on content cards, grain overlays, the ghost background words, duplicate CTA blocks.

**Brought back from the live site (owner favourites):** the centred hero with per-word rise and the glowing *online*; the decode/scramble text effect; the footer's dot-matrix logo that assembles and reacts to the cursor. These become the seeds of the Living Mark and the hero.

## 8. Positioning (what we say, and what we never say)

**We say:** Nepal's GPU cloud, built on NVIDIA H200 in the Kathmandu Valley, with HPE and NVIDIA. Your data stays in the valley. Billed by the second, in rupees. Engineers in Kathmandu, on Nepal time. Starting now, built for the long run.

**We never say:** "Nepal's first" (a competitor launched in July 2026), "100% renewable" (the grid is hydro-dominated but imports power in the dry season), "available now" (the H200 is coming soon, enterprise early access), "NRB-approved"/"DoIT-listed"/"Tier III" unless and until it is true, "AI factory", "superintelligence", or any number we have not measured. Full list in [04-content-and-facts.md](04-content-and-facts.md).

## 9. Ambition, stated carefully

We start with one HPE Private Cloud AI cluster: eight NVIDIA H200 NVL GPUs. The site must feel like the beginning of something much larger — new GPU generations as they ship, larger clusters, one day our own datacenter — **without promising any of it**. The company page carries the long-range vision as vision ("where we're going"), clearly separated from what is true today ("what runs now").

## 10. Definition of done for the whole project

- Every page passes the "one owner per fact" check and the claims guardrails.
- Lighthouse (mobile) ≥ 90 performance, 100 accessibility, 100 best practices, 100 SEO on every marketing route; field LCP ≤ 2.0 s, INP ≤ 150 ms, CLS ≤ 0.05.
- WCAG 2.2 AA throughout; complete reduced-motion and keyboard paths.
- Smooth on the device matrix (low-end Android, mid Android, iPhone non-Pro, iPhone Pro, Windows laptop iGPU, MacBook): no dropped-frame storms during scroll, no thermal throttling in a 5-minute soak.
- Both themes complete and consistent; no flash of the wrong theme.
- The owner can look at any screen and say which of the three visual ideas it uses and which fact it carries.
